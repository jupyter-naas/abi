The Compliance Angle

ISO 27001 cannot certify on top of weak data foundations

  • Asset inventory (A.5.9) requires knowing what data exists and who owns it.
  • Access control (A.8.2–A.8.5) requires clear data ownership to define who should have access.
  • Information classification (A.5.12) requires a common taxonomy across the organization.
  • Internal audit evidence depends on documented, enforced data policies — not informal practice.

Best Practices · Foundations

Governance rests on three pillars: people, process, technology

People — “Who”
  • Executive sponsorship at the highest level
  • Data Owners & Data Stewards with real authority
  • Clear roles, not just a RACI on paper
Process — “How”
  • Policies & standards consistently enforced
  • Issue escalation and resolution workflow
  • Change management built in from day one
Technology — “What”
  • A corporate, single view of data
  • Metrics dashboards and DQ monitoring
  • Access controls aligned to data classification

Data Governance is an ongoing program, not a project — it must be reviewed, funded and reinforced year over year.

Best Practices · Operating Model

Three governance models, one clear winner for ISO 27001

Limited success

Decentralized

Business units control their own data independently.

Inconsistent definitions and controls — hard to pass a unified audit.

Recommended

Federated

Central standards, local execution — shared accountability.

Balances ISO 27001’s need for central control with day-to-day business ownership of data.

Effective, but rigid

Centralized

Single point of control; little business-unit autonomy.

Strong control, but slower and can disengage the business.

The federated model gives auditors the central control they require while keeping the business engaged and accountable for its own data.

Timeliness

Comparability

Fields stay consistent over time, enabling clean reporting & audit trails.

What Good Looks Like · Metrics

What we’ll measure — and report to both the business and the auditor
