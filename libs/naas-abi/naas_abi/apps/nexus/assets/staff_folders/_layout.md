## How this drive is organized

This workspace drive follows the **staff system**, the way most headquarters divide their work into
nine functions. A folder answers one question, whatever the job title of the person filing in it,
so the same layout fits a bank, a lab or a startup and anyone can guess where a file lives.

| Folder | Function | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| `personnel/` | S1 | Who is in the organization, and what is their status? |
| `intelligence/` | S2 | What is true about the world outside us? |
| `operations/` | S3 | How do we execute the mission we are on right now? |
| `logistics/` | S4 | What physical or contracted resources do we need, and where are they? |
| `plans/` | S5 | What should we do next, and how will we get there? |
| `signals/` | S6 | How does information move and get stored inside the organization? |
| `training/` | S7 | How do our people become capable of their work? |
| `finance/` | S8 | Where does the money go, and what is it worth? |
| `external/` | S9 | How do we relate to people and bodies outside the organization? |

**Filing rule:** file by what the work *is*, not by which team produced it. If a file seems to fit
two folders, it usually holds two things: split it. If it fits none, that is worth raising, since an
empty folder shows a function the workspace does not cover yet.

The most common mix-up is **operations vs plans**: operations is *now*, plans is *next*. This
quarter's campaign belongs in `operations/`; next year's belongs in `plans/`.
